\section*{A test that separates the models}
New scenes can separate the models where the 20 videos cannot. In the three kinds of scene built to compare two models (first three rows), the pair differs by 41 to 47 rating points on average, against at most $\pm 12$ points (95\% interval) of noise in a video's mean rating in the original data. In all 24 the budget model says A did not matter; the budget-size scenes, where it says A mattered in half (82.8), guard against a general lean toward that answer.

Fixed rules, not hand-picking, chose the scenes. We generated 500,000 random scenes in the style of the originals (3 to 9 balls) and kept the 37,167 that meet the original videos' constraints: B goes in, 7 to 13 seconds, every collision on screen, and no two collisions close in time and place. A scene qualifies when two models' (or two budgets') predicted probabilities differ by at least 40 points at every plausible setting: budgets of 1 to 3 balls, and every noise level whose fit is within the best fit's 95\% interval.

Simple visual cues do not give the answers away, and simulated studies recover the true model. After rule-based swaps, across the 32 new scenes no cue (A's closest approach to B, the number of collisions or balls) correlates with the gap between the budget model and any rival, or between budget sizes, above $|r| = .3$ (largest .26). In 200 simulated studies per model, that model (the generating model) produced the ratings of 50 simulated people, each copying one real participant's slider use and noise, and we refit all models blind. The generating model always fit best, and a budget model's studies were always fit best by its own $k$. A mix of budgets in the per-person proportions was fit best by the budget model in all 200, with $k = 2$ in 199. These studies assume each person follows one model, so real data will separate the models less cleanly.

We propose running the 36 scenes as in Lu et al.'s Experiment 1 (about 27 minutes per participant). The budget model passes if its held-out error beats every rival's with a 95\% interval excluding zero, fails if any rival beats it that way, and is otherwise inconclusive. The videos, predictions and analysis plan are in our repository, ready for your lab to run; we welcome changes.
